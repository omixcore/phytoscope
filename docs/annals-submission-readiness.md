# Annals submission readiness audit (internal; not manuscript text)

This file is an evidence and compliance checklist for the authors. It is not
draft article text. It must not be copied into a submission as manuscript
prose.

## Journal requirements checked on 2026-10-19

The current *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* author guidelines list
Original Articles at up to 7,000 words and 100 references. The main document
requires an informative title, author names and affiliations, an unstructured
abstract of no more than 200 words, up to seven keywords, a graphical abstract
(70 words maximum or a suggested figure), Introduction, Materials & Methods,
Results, Discussion, Conclusion, acknowledgments, author contributions,
competing-interest statement, references, and figure legends. Figures and
editable tables are uploaded separately; supporting information is a separate
file. The journal requires a data-availability statement and ORCID identifiers.

**AI policy blocker:** the author guidelines state that text, figures, images,
or graphics generated by AI/ML tools cannot be used in an Annals paper without
explicit editor permission. AI-assisted copy editing is allowed only for
author-generated text and limited to readability, grammar, spelling,
punctuation, and tone. No manuscript prose or submission figures have been
generated as part of this audit. Before using AI-generated material, authors
must obtain explicit permission from the editorial office and follow its
instructions. Source: https://nyaspubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/hub/journal/17496632/author-guidelines.html

The journal describes its remit as high-impact primary research, reviews, and
perspectives that advance understanding in a field and, where possible,
resonate beyond it. Editorial fit is therefore not established by software
validation alone. Source: https://nyaspubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/hub/journal/17496632/aims-and-scope.html

## Recommended manuscript organization (headings only)

1. Introduction: information-access gap for portal-poor plant species; limits
   of species-specific databases; study question and bounded contribution.
2. Materials & Methods: software architecture and supported inputs; versioned
   public datasets; source-derived gold construction; test tasks and independent
   comparators; expression re-quantification; software/CI environment; claim
   boundaries.
3. Results: shared-code portability; exact structural/sequence agreement;
   public expression reconciliation; matched parser comparison; bounded
   research-module checks; project recovery and reproducibility.
4. Discussion: what is reusable for non-model species; what remains dependent on
   user-supplied genome, annotation, expression and metadata; how this differs
   from a species database or general-purpose genome browser; limitations.
5. Conclusion: tested scope only; no user-efficiency, causal-biology, or novel
   discovery claim.
